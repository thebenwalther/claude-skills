# Leverage Questions

**Leverage is what makes change inevitable.**

When people say "I know I should but I just can't," they don't have a knowledge problem — they have a leverage problem. The pain of staying the same hasn't exceeded the pain of changing.

These questions create leverage by making the consequences of inaction vivid and real.

---

## The Leverage Formula

**Change happens when:**
Pain of staying the same > Pain of changing
AND
Pleasure of changing > Pleasure of staying the same

Most people have vague dissatisfaction but vivid fears of change. These questions flip that — making the cost of inaction vivid and the rewards of action compelling.

---

## Question Categories

### Pain Questions (Create Urgency)

Associate massive pain with NOT changing.

**Short-term pain:**
- "What is this costing you right now — energy, time, money, relationships?"
- "How does this affect you on a daily basis?"
- "What do you miss out on because of this?"

**Long-term pain (The Rocking Chair Method):**
- "If nothing changes, what will your life look like in 5 years?"
- "Imagine you're 80, looking back. If you never changed this, what would you regret?"
- "What will you never achieve if you keep this pattern?"
- "Who will you never become?"
- "What will you have to tell yourself to justify not acting?"

**Impact on others:**
- "Who else is affected by this? What's it costing them?"
- "If your children/loved ones copied this exact behavior, how would you feel?"
- "What example are you setting by not changing?"

**Identity pain:**
- "What does staying in this pattern say about who you are?"
- "Is this the person you want to be?"
- "What would the best version of you think about this?"

### Pleasure Questions (Create Pull)

Associate massive pleasure with changing.

**Vision of success:**
- "Who will you become if you change this?"
- "What becomes possible that isn't possible now?"
- "What would your life look like in 5 years if you made this change today?"

**Emotional rewards:**
- "How would you feel about yourself if you finally conquered this?"
- "What would waking up without this problem feel like?"
- "Who would be proud of you for making this change?"

**Ripple effects:**
- "What else would improve as a side effect of this change?"
- "How would your relationships be different?"
- "What would you finally have the energy/time/confidence to pursue?"

## The Rocking Chair Method (Full Version)

This is the most powerful leverage technique. Use when someone is deeply stuck.

**The Setup:**
> "I want you to imagine something. You're 80 years old, sitting in a rocking chair, looking back on your life."

**Part 1: The Unlived Life**
> "In this version, you never changed this. You kept the same pattern for the next [X] years.
>
> Looking back from that rocking chair:
> - What did you never do?
> - Who did you never become?
> - What do you regret most?
> - What will you wish you had done differently?
>
> Really feel that. The weight of decades of this pattern."

**Part 2: The Life You Want**
> "Now, new scenario. Same rocking chair, but this time you DID change. You made the shift.
>
> Looking back:
> - What did that change make possible?
> - Who did you become?
> - What are you most proud of?
> - What advice would that version of you give the you who's sitting here right now?"

**The Close:**
> "Which rocking chair do you want to be sitting in?"

## When to Use

**Trigger phrases:**
- "I know I should but..."
- "I've been meaning to..."
- "I keep putting it off..."
- "I want to but I just can't get started"
- "I'm not motivated enough"
- "I'll start Monday / next month / when..."

**How to offer:**
> "Sounds like you know what to do — the motivation just isn't there yet. Want me to ask a few questions that might help create some leverage?"

If they say yes, proceed. If they're not ready, don't push.

---

## Response Format

When someone agrees to leverage questions:

```
[Brief setup — what we're doing and why]

**Question 1:** [Pain-focused question]

**Question 2:** [Deeper pain — consequences over time]

**Question 3:** [Pleasure-focused — who they become]

[Optional: Closing question that prompts action]
```

## Example

**User:** "I know I need to start exercising. I keep saying I will but I never actually do. I've been meaning to get back in shape for like two years now."

**Response:**

Let's create some leverage. I'm going to ask you a few questions — really sit with them.

**Question 1:** What has not exercising already cost you in these two years? Energy, confidence, how you feel in your body, how you show up in other areas?

**Question 2:** Imagine it's 5 years from now and nothing's changed. You still haven't started. You're 5 years older, 5 years more deconditioned, 5 years of "I'll start Monday." How does that feel?

**Question 3:** Now flip it. You DID start — today. In 5 years, you're in the best shape of your life. You have energy, confidence, vitality. What becomes possible? Who do you become?

**The real question:** The pain of starting exists either way. Do you want to feel it now and get the payoff? Or keep avoiding it and feel the pain of regret instead?

What's ONE thing you could do today — even for 10 minutes — to prove to yourself you're someone who takes action?

---

## Important Notes

- **Don't rapid-fire questions.** Let them sit with each one. Silence is good.
- **The goal is emotional activation.** If they're answering intellectually, go deeper.
- **Pain is the sharper lever.** Humans are more motivated to avoid pain than to gain pleasure. Use pain questions first.
- **Don't create leverage for unimportant things.** This is for meaningful changes, not "should I buy the blue shirt or the green one."
- **Follow leverage with action.** Leverage without immediate action fades quickly. Always end with: "What will you do in the next 24 hours?"
