# Seven Forces Business Audit

Every business problem lives in one of seven areas. When you're stuck, overwhelmed, or spinning wheels, it's because you're either:
1. Ignoring the real bottleneck while working on something else
2. Trying to fix everything at once

This audit identifies the ONE force that's your current constraint and prescribes the focused action that will have disproportionate impact.

---

## The Seven Forces

### 1. Create an Effective Business Map
**The strategy force.** Vision, mission, positioning, competitive advantage, and strategic plan.

**Healthy looks like:**
- Clear vision everyone can articulate
- Differentiated positioning (not competing on price)
- Strategy that creates sustainable advantage
- Business model that works at scale

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- No clear direction ("we do a bit of everything")
- Competing as a commodity
- Strategy shifts constantly
- Can't explain why customers should choose you

**Key question:** "Can everyone in the business explain in one sentence why customers choose you over alternatives?"

---

### 2. Financial Mastery
**The numbers force.** Cash flow, profitability, financial systems, and capital allocation.

**Healthy looks like:**
- Profitable with healthy margins
- Cash flow positive and predictable
- Clear understanding of unit economics
- Financial reporting you actually use
- Capital deployed strategically

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Revenue going up, profit staying flat
- Constant cash crunches
- No idea which products/clients are profitable
- Making decisions without financial data
- Owner pulling money randomly

**Key question:** "Do you know your customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, and margin per product/service line — and check them monthly?"

---

### 3. Consistent Sales Mastery
**The revenue force.** Sales process, conversion, pipeline, and revenue predictability.

**Healthy looks like:**
- Predictable, repeatable sales process
- Strong pipeline of qualified leads
- High conversion rates at each stage
- Sales not dependent on founder/owner
- Referrals generating significant revenue

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Feast or famine revenue
- No defined sales process
- Leads falling through cracks
- Only the founder can close deals
- Discounting to win business

**Key question:** "If you removed yourself from sales for 90 days, would revenue stay steady?"

---

### 4. World-Class Marketing
**The attention force.** Brand, messaging, lead generation, and market positioning.

**Healthy looks like:**
- Clear brand that stands for something
- Marketing that attracts ideal clients (not just any clients)
- Multiple working lead generation channels
- Content/assets working while you sleep
- Marketing ROI is measurable

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Relying on one channel (referrals only, or just paid ads)
- Marketing feels like guessing
- Attracting wrong-fit clients
- No brand differentiation
- Can't attribute revenue to marketing spend

**Key question:** "What percentage of your revenue comes from proactive marketing vs. passive word-of-mouth?"

---

### 5. Systems & Operations Excellence
**The efficiency force.** Processes, technology, delivery, and operational leverage.

**Healthy looks like:**
- Documented processes anyone can follow
- Technology reducing manual work
- Quality delivery without founder involvement
- Operations scale without proportional headcount
- Errors and rework are rare

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Everything in founder's head
- Same mistakes repeated
- Delivery quality varies wildly
- Scaling means linear headcount increase
- Fighting fires constantly

**Key question:** "Could your business deliver its core product/service at 80% quality if you disappeared for 30 days?"

---

### 6. Leadership & Culture
**The people force.** Team, talent, culture, development, and organizational effectiveness.

**Healthy looks like:**
- A-players who own outcomes
- Culture people want to be part of
- Low regrettable turnover
- Leadership pipeline developing
- People growing faster than the business needs

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Founder doing everything important
- Hiring for skills, ignoring values fit
- Good people leaving
- Culture not explicitly defined
- People management is reactive

**Key question:** "Do you have at least one person who could run operations if you stepped back for a month?"

---

### 7. Customer Loyalty & Raving Fans
**The retention force.** Customer experience, satisfaction, retention, and advocacy.

**Healthy looks like:**
- High retention / low churn
- Customers become advocates
- Net Promoter Score consistently high
- Customers expand (buy more over time)
- Feedback loop informing product/service

**Unhealthy looks like:**
- Churn offsetting new sales
- No proactive customer success
- Don't know why customers leave
- Referrals are rare
- Same complaints repeating

**Key question:** "What percentage of your customers actively refer new business without being asked?"

## The Audit Process

### Step 1: Quick Assessment

For each force, rate current state 1-10:

| Force | Rating (1-10) | Primary Issue |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| 1. Business Map / Strategy | | |
| 2. Financial Mastery | | |
| 3. Sales Mastery | | |
| 4. Marketing | | |
| 5. Systems & Operations | | |
| 6. Leadership & Culture | | |
| 7. Customer Loyalty | | |

### Step 2: Identify the Bottleneck

**The bottleneck is NOT always the lowest score.** It's the force that, if improved, would unlock the others.

**Decision tree:**
1. If you don't have clarity on strategy and positioning → **Force 1** (nothing else matters until this is clear)
2. If you're not profitable or running out of cash → **Force 2** (survival comes first)
3. If you have a great product but not enough customers → **Force 3 or 4** (sales or marketing)
4. If you're growing but chaos is increasing → **Force 5** (systems are the constraint)
5. If you have too much on your plate → **Force 6** (you need people)
6. If you're acquiring customers but losing them → **Force 7** (leaky bucket)

**Ask:** "If we magically fixed this ONE force, would the others get easier?"

### Step 3: Find the 2% Focus

Within the bottleneck force, identify the specific constraint — the smallest intervention that would create the biggest shift.

**Not:** "We need to fix our marketing"
**But:** "We need to create one lead magnet that actually converts for our ideal customer"

**Not:** "We need better systems"
**But:** "We need to document the client onboarding process so it doesn't require me"

### Step 4: Define the 90-Day Play

One force, one focus, one measurable outcome.

## Example

**User:** "My consulting business is doing $400K revenue but I'm working 60-hour weeks and can't seem to break past this level."

### Force Assessment

| Force | Rating | Status |
|-------|--------|--------|
| 1. Business Map | 7/10 | Clear positioning, decent differentiation |
| 2. Financial Mastery | 6/10 | Profitable but margins could be better |
| 3. Sales Mastery | 5/10 | Too dependent on founder for closing |
| 4. Marketing | 6/10 | Referrals working, but no proactive lead gen |
| 5. Systems & Operations | 3/10 | Everything in founder's head |
| 6. Leadership & Culture | 4/10 | Solo + contractors, no real team |
| 7. Customer Loyalty | 8/10 | Great retention, clients love the work |

### The Bottleneck

**Primary constraint:** Force 5 — Systems & Operations

**Why this is the bottleneck:**
You can't hire people (Force 6) because nothing is documented — they'd need you for everything. You can't sell more (Force 3) because you're maxed out delivering. You're trading time for money with no leverage. Every hour requires your presence.

**Evidence:**
- You're the only one who can deliver the core service
- No SOPs exist — everything lives in your head
- Adding clients means adding your hours
- You haven't taken a real vacation in 2 years

### The 2% Focus

**The specific constraint:** You don't have a documented delivery process that someone else could execute.

**What's NOT the focus:** Sales improvement, marketing, hiring, new service lines — all matter but are blocked until this is solved.

### The 90-Day Play

**Outcome:** Have one contractor deliver 3 client engagements at 85%+ quality without your involvement in execution (only QA).

**Key actions:**
1. Document your client delivery process — every step, every template, every decision tree
2. Hire one senior contractor and train them using the documentation
3. Have them shadow you for 2 engagements, then flip it (you shadow them)
4. Measure quality and client satisfaction to validate

**Leading indicator:** Hours of your time per client engagement (should decrease week over week)

**Lagging indicator:** Client satisfaction scores remain ≥ 8/10 on contractor-delivered work

### What This Unlocks

Once operations are documented and delegated:
- You can hire more delivery capacity (Force 6)
- You can sell more without burning out (Force 3)
- You can take real time off
- Revenue can grow without 1:1 relationship to your hours

---

## Important Notes

- **Don't work on all seven at once.** One bottleneck at a time.
- **The bottleneck shifts.** Once you fix one, another becomes the constraint. That's progress.
- **2% focus beats 50% effort.** The smallest intervention with the biggest leverage beats broad effort.
- **Diagnosis must be honest.** The founder often wants to work on their favorite force, not the actual bottleneck.
- **Revisit quarterly.** Business evolves. The bottleneck 6 months ago may not be the bottleneck today.
