# Values Hierarchy Clarifier

You make every decision based on your values — whether you're conscious of them or not.

When decisions feel impossible, it's usually because two values are in conflict and you don't know which one ranks higher. When you keep making choices you regret, it's usually because you're honoring a value you didn't know you had.

This process makes the invisible hierarchy visible.

---

## Why Values Hierarchies Matter

**Your values hierarchy determines your life.**

- If Freedom ranks above Security, you'll quit stable jobs for uncertainty
- If Security ranks above Freedom, you'll stay in golden handcuffs forever
- If Significance ranks above Connection, you'll achieve alone
- If Connection ranks above Significance, you might suppress ambition to belong

**Most internal conflicts are values conflicts.** You feel torn because two things you value are pulling in opposite directions. The resolution isn't to pick one forever — it's to understand which takes precedence in THIS context.

---

## The Process

### Step 1: Elicit Values

Ask: "What's most important to you in life? What do you value above all else?"

**Prompt with domains if needed:**
- "What's most important to you in your career/work?"
- "What's most important to you in relationships?"
- "What's most important to you for your health/body?"
- "What's most important to you in how you spend your time?"

**Common values (for reference, don't just list these):**
Freedom, Security, Adventure, Stability, Love, Connection, Significance, Achievement, Growth, Contribution, Health, Wealth, Family, Creativity, Independence, Recognition, Peace, Excitement, Authenticity, Power, Respect, Faith, Learning, Fun, Integrity, Success, Balance, Passion, Impact, Legacy

**Dig beneath surface answers:**
- If they say "money," ask "What does money give you?" (Often Security or Freedom)
- If they say "success," ask "What does success mean to you?" (Often Significance or Achievement)
- If they say "happiness," ask "What makes you happy specifically?"

**Get 8-12 values.** Fewer than 8 is probably surface-level. More than 12 is hard to rank.

### Step 2: Define Each Value

For each value, ask: "What does [value] mean to you specifically?"

**This is crucial.** "Freedom" to one person means "work from anywhere." To another it means "never answer to a boss." To another it means "financial independence."

Get their personal definition, not the dictionary's.

### Step 3: Rank the Values

This is where the insight happens.

**Method 1: Forced Comparison**
Take the top two values. Ask:
> "If you could only have [Value A] OR [Value B] — not both — which would you choose?"

The one they choose ranks higher. Compare the winner against the next value. Continue until all are ranked.

**Method 2: Life Proof**
Look at their actual decisions:
> "Think about a big decision you made recently. Which value did you honor? Which did you sacrifice?"

Actual behavior reveals actual hierarchy better than hypotheticals.

**Method 3: Nightmare Scenario**
For close calls:
> "Imagine a life where you have [Value A] but completely lack [Value B]. Now imagine the reverse. Which nightmare is worse?"

The one they can't tolerate losing ranks higher.

### Step 4: Identify Conflicts

Look at the ranked list and ask:
> "Where do you see potential conflicts between these values?"
> "Have you ever had to choose between two of these?"

**Common conflict patterns:**
- Freedom vs. Security (adventure vs. stability)
- Significance vs. Connection (standing out vs. belonging)
- Growth vs. Comfort (pushing edges vs. peace)
- Family vs. Career (presence vs. achievement)
- Authenticity vs. Acceptance (being yourself vs. fitting in)

### Step 5: Reveal Unconscious Values

Sometimes the most powerful values are the ones we don't admit.

**Look for:**
- Values they keep honoring but didn't list (e.g., keeps choosing Comfort but listed Growth as #1)
- Values they judge in others (often projected)
- Values they're afraid to admit (Significance, Power, Wealth)

> "Is there a value you think you *should* have lower but keeps showing up in your decisions?"

### Step 6: Check for "Toward" vs. "Away" Orientation

Each value can be pursued positively (toward pleasure) or negatively (away from pain):

- Security (toward): "I want to feel safe and stable"
- Security (away): "I never want to feel vulnerable or at risk"

The "away from" version often drives behavior more urgently but less sustainably.

> "For [Value], are you moving toward something you want? Or away from something you fear?"

## Example

**User:** "I keep taking jobs that pay well but make me miserable, then quitting after a year. I've done this four times."

**Elicited values:** Freedom, Security, Achievement, Growth, Connection, Wealth, Health, Adventure

**After ranking and analysis:**

### Top Values (Ranked)

| Rank | Value | Definition | Orientation |
|------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | Freedom | Autonomy over my time and decisions | Toward |
| 2 | Security | Financial stability, never being broke | Away from |
| 3 | Achievement | Doing excellent work I'm proud of | Toward |
| 4 | Growth | Learning, becoming more capable | Toward |
| 5 | Wealth | Having more than enough money | Toward |
| 6 | Connection | Deep relationships, belonging | Toward |
| 7 | Adventure | New experiences, excitement | Toward |
| 8 | Health | Physical energy and longevity | Toward |

### Key Conflict Identified

**Freedom (#1) vs. Security (#2)**

This is your core tension. Freedom is your highest value — you need autonomy. But Security is "away from" — you're terrified of being broke. So you take high-paying jobs (Security), then become miserable because they constrain your Freedom. You quit (Freedom wins), then panic about money (Security screams), and take another corporate job.

**Resolution insight:** You don't need to pick one. You need to redefine Security. Right now Security = "high salary from employer." But Security could = "$X in savings + diversified income streams." That version of Security doesn't conflict with Freedom — it enables it.

### The Insight

You keep optimizing for Security in ways that violate Freedom. Then Freedom wins, you quit, and you're back at zero Security. The cycle breaks when you build Security in a Freedom-compatible way — savings, skills, multiple income sources — not through dependence on a single employer.

### One Decision This Clarifies

Your next career move shouldn't be "highest paying job available." It should be "sustainable income that preserves autonomy while I build a 12-month runway." The number might be lower, but it honors both values.

---

## Important Notes

- **Actual behavior reveals actual values.** What people say they value and what they actually value often differ.
- **Values aren't fixed forever.** They can shift with life stages. Someone with young kids might temporarily elevate Security/Family.
- **There are no wrong values.** Don't judge Significance or Wealth. They're human. The question is whether they're being met constructively.
- **Conflicts are information.** They're not problems to solve but tensions to understand and navigate consciously.
- **"Away from" values need attention.** They're often fear-driven and can hijack decisions. Naming them reduces their power.
