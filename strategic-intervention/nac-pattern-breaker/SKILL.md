# NAC Pattern Breaker

Neuro-Associative Conditioning is Tony Robbins' systematic approach to breaking unwanted patterns and installing new ones. It's based on one principle:

**We don't do what's logical. We do what we've linked pain and pleasure to.**

Every behavior you can't stop is something your nervous system has linked to pleasure (or pain avoidance). Every behavior you can't start is something linked to pain (or pleasure loss). NAC rewires these associations.

---

## The 6 Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Target Pattern

Get specific about what needs to change.

**Questions:**
- "What is the exact behavior you want to change?"
- "When does it happen? What triggers it?"
- "How long have you had this pattern?"
- "What have you tried before to change it?"

**Get concrete.** Not "I procrastinate" but "When I sit down to work on my taxes, I open YouTube instead." Not "I'm bad with money" but "When I feel stressed, I buy things online I don't need."

**Identify the trigger-behavior-reward loop:**
- Trigger: What cue initiates the pattern?
- Behavior: What do you actually do?
- Reward: What need does this meet? (Link to Six Human Needs)

### Step 2: Get Leverage

This is the most important step. Without massive leverage, change won't stick.

**Leverage = Pain of staying the same > Pain of changing**

Most people have mild dissatisfaction with their patterns. That's not enough. You need to make the cost of this pattern unbearable.

**Pain questions (link massive pain to the old pattern):**
- "What has this pattern already cost you? Be specific."
- "What will it cost you over the next 5 years if nothing changes?"
- "Who else is affected by this pattern? What does it cost them?"
- "What does this pattern say about who you are? Is that who you want to be?"
- "If your children/loved ones copied this exact pattern, how would you feel?"

**Pleasure questions (link massive pleasure to the new pattern):**
- "Who would you become if you broke this pattern?"
- "What would be possible in your life that isn't possible now?"
- "How would the people who love you benefit?"
- "What would it mean about you if you finally conquered this?"

**The test:** If they're not feeling significant emotional activation, you don't have enough leverage. Go deeper. Use specific examples. Make it vivid.

**Write a leverage statement:** Have them write 2-3 sentences capturing the pain of continuing and the pleasure of changing. This becomes their go-to reminder.

### Step 3: Interrupt the Pattern

Patterns run on autopilot. You need to scramble the signal before you can install a new program.

**Pattern interrupts work by doing something so unexpected that the brain can't run the old sequence.**

**Types of pattern interrupts:**

**Physical:**
- Clap hands loudly
- Stand up and shake entire body
- Cold water on face
- 20 jumping jacks
- Change rooms immediately
- Yell a nonsense word

**Mental:**
- Rapid counting backwards from 100 by 7s
- Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can hear, 3 you can feel
- Ask an absurd question: "What would a penguin do right now?"

**Pattern Interrupt Examples for Common Loops:**
| Pattern | Interrupt |
|---------|-----------|
| About to stress-eat | Put ice cubes in mouth, hold 30 seconds |
| Opening social media mindlessly | Stand up, do 10 squats, then decide if you still want to |
| Negative self-talk spiral | Say "STOP" out loud, clap, state one thing going well |
| Procrastination paralysis | 5-4-3-2-1 and stand up immediately |

**The key:** The interrupt must happen at the moment of the trigger, before the behavior starts. Identify the earliest possible warning sign and tie the interrupt to that.

### Step 4: Install the Replacement

Nature abhors a vacuum. If you remove a pattern without installing a replacement, the old pattern comes back or something worse fills the void.

**The replacement must meet the same needs as the old pattern** — just in a constructive way.

**Questions:**
- "What needs was the old pattern meeting?" (Use Needs Decoder if unclear)
- "What's a healthier behavior that could meet those same needs?"
- "What will you do instead?"

**Make it specific:**
- Wrong: "I'll be more productive instead of scrolling"
- Right: "When I feel the urge to scroll, I'll write one sentence of my project"

**The replacement should:**
- Be easy to do (low friction)
- Meet at least some of the same needs
- Have its own reward built in

**Create an if-then plan:**
> "If [trigger], then I will [interrupt] and then [replacement behavior]."

Example:
> "If I feel the urge to check my phone in bed, I will put my phone across the room and do 5 deep breaths, then read one page of my book."

### Step 5: Condition the New Pattern

A new behavior needs repetition to become automatic. This is conditioning.

**Methods:**

**1. Massive Action (Immersion)**
Do the new behavior intensively for a short period. Don't gradually transition — go all in for 7-30 days.

**2. Reinforcement**
Every time you do the new behavior, create a positive association:
- Internal celebration ("YES! I did it!")
- Physical gesture (fist pump, smile)
- Small reward (but not the old pattern)

**3. Identity linking**
Connect the new behavior to who you are:
> "This is what I do now. I'm the kind of person who [new behavior]."

**4. Social accountability**
Tell someone who will check in on you. Public commitment strengthens private resolve.

**5. Environment design**
Make the old pattern hard and the new pattern easy:
- Remove triggers for old pattern
- Add triggers for new pattern
- Change the environment where old pattern occurred

### Step 6: Future Pace

Lock in the change by vividly rehearsing success.

**The prompt:**
> "Close your eyes. See yourself one week from now, having successfully used this new pattern every single time. How do you feel?

> See yourself one month from now. This new behavior is becoming automatic. What's different in your life?

> See yourself one year from now. The old pattern is a distant memory. You barely remember what it felt like to struggle with this. What has this change made possible?"

**Then:**
> "See yourself facing the trigger situation tomorrow. See yourself executing the interrupt, then the replacement. See yourself feeling proud. Run that movie in your head until it feels natural."

## Example

**User:** "I keep checking my phone first thing in the morning. I wake up and before I even get out of bed, I'm scrolling Instagram for 30 minutes. I hate how it starts my day."

### The Pattern to Break
**Trigger:** Waking up, phone within reach
**Behavior:** Grab phone, open Instagram, scroll 30+ min
**Needs Met:** Variety (novelty/stimulation), Connection (seeing what people posted), Certainty (familiar comfort before facing the day)

### Leverage Statement
"Every morning I give away my first 30 minutes — my freshest, clearest thinking — to other people's highlight reels. Over a year that's 182 hours of my best energy fed to an algorithm. My mornings shape my days, and my days shape my life. I'm choosing to start with intention, not addiction."

### Pattern Interrupt
**Warning sign:** Hand reaching for phone
**Interrupt:** Immediately say "NOT FIRST" out loud, roll out of bed opposite side, feet on floor

### The Replacement
**If-Then Plan:** If I wake up and want my phone, I will say "NOT FIRST," get out of bed, walk to kitchen, drink full glass of water, and spend 5 minutes journaling or stretching. Phone stays in another room until after this.

### Conditioning Plan
- [x] Immersion: 21 days, zero phone until after morning routine complete
- [x] Reinforcement: Fist pump and "I OWN MY MORNING" after each successful morning
- [x] Identity: "I'm the kind of person who controls my attention"
- [x] Accountability: Text partner by 8am with "Morning routine done ✓"
- [x] Environment: Phone charges in kitchen, not bedroom. Book/journal on nightstand.

## Important Notes

- **Leverage is everything.** If step 2 isn't emotionally charged, steps 3-6 won't stick.
- **The replacement must meet the same needs.** Otherwise you're fighting human nature.
- **Conditioning takes time.** 21-66 days for a new habit to become automatic. Expect the old pattern to fight back around days 3-7 and 14-21.
- **Environment > willpower.** Redesigning the environment is more reliable than relying on motivation.
- **Slip-ups aren't failure.** A slip is data. What triggered it? What was the need? Adjust and continue.
