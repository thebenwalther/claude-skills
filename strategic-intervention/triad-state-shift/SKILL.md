# Triad State Shift

**State determines everything.** When you're in a strong state, problems feel manageable, actions feel possible, and your best self shows up. In a weak state, everything feels harder than it is.

**The Triad:** Your emotional state is created by three things, and changing any one changes the state.

1. **Physiology** — How you're using your body
2. **Focus** — What you're paying attention to
3. **Language** — What you're saying to yourself

---

## Physiology (The Fastest Lever)

**Change the body → change the state.** This works in seconds.

**Immediate interventions:**
- Stand up and move (even just walking to another room)
- 20 jumping jacks or 10 pushups
- Cold water on face or wrists
- 5 deep breaths: 4 seconds in, hold 4, out 8
- Power pose: stand tall, hands on hips or arms overhead, 60 seconds
- Shake your entire body vigorously for 30 seconds
- Change location — go outside if possible

**Posture check:**
- Are you collapsed or upright?
- Shoulders rolled forward or back?
- Breathing shallow or deep?
- Face tense or relaxed?

**The rule:** You can't feel depressed with excellent posture, deep breathing, and movement. The physiology won't allow it.

---

## Focus (What You're Paying Attention To)

**What's wrong is always available. So is what's right.**

**Shift focus with questions:**
- "What am I grateful for right now?" (not conceptually — actually feel it)
- "What's working that I'm ignoring?"
- "What would I focus on if I felt confident about this?"
- "What's the opportunity here that I'm missing?"
- "In one year, how big will this problem feel?"

**Focus patterns that destroy state:**
- Catastrophizing the future
- Ruminating on the past
- Focusing on what you can't control
- Comparing yourself to others
- Looking for evidence that confirms your fears

**Focus patterns that build state:**
- Gratitude (specific, not generic)
- What's in your control right now
- Small next steps, not the whole mountain
- Progress made, not distance remaining
- Who you're becoming, not where you fell short

## Language (What You're Saying to Yourself)

**Internal dialogue creates internal experience.**

**Reframes:**
| Weak Language | Strong Language |
|---------------|-----------------|
| "I have to" | "I get to" / "I choose to" |
| "I can't" | "I haven't yet" / "I'm figuring out how" |
| "This is terrible" | "This is challenging" / "This is temporary" |
| "I'm overwhelmed" | "I have a lot happening" / "I need to prioritize" |
| "I failed" | "I learned something" / "I got feedback" |
| "I'm so stressed" | "I'm activated" / "There's a lot I care about" |
| "Why me?" | "What can I learn?" / "What's this asking of me?" |

**Power questions to ask yourself:**
- "What would the best version of me do right now?"
- "What's one thing I can do in the next 5 minutes?"
- "How do I want to feel, and what would create that?"

## Quick State Shift Protocol (2-3 minutes)

When someone needs a fast shift:

### Step 1: Physiology Reset (60 seconds)
Stand up. Do 5 deep breaths (in 4, hold 4, out 8). Shake your body out. Stand tall, shoulders back. Smile (even if forced — the physiology affects the psychology).

### Step 2: Focus Shift (60 seconds)
Answer quickly: "What's ONE thing going right that you're taking for granted?"
Then: "What's ONE thing you're looking forward to in the next 24 hours?"

### Step 3: Language Upgrade (30 seconds)
Take the feeling you described (stressed, anxious, overwhelmed) and reframe it:
- "Stressed" → "I have a lot I care about right now"
- "Overwhelmed" → "I have more opportunities than I've prioritized"
- "Anxious" → "I'm activated because this matters"

Say the reframe out loud once.

### Step 4: One Action
From this new state: "What's one small action you could take in the next 10 minutes that would create momentum?"

## State Shift Response Format

When someone expresses weak state, respond concisely:

```
Sounds like you're in a tough state. Here's a quick reset:

**Physiology:** [Specific physical intervention — 1-2 sentences]

**Focus:** [One question to shift focus]

**Language:** Instead of "[their word]," try: "[reframe]"

**One action:** [Tiny next step to create momentum]

[Optional: brief encouragement]
```

## Example

**User:** "I'm so overwhelmed. I have so much to do and I don't even know where to start. I just feel paralyzed."

**Response:**

Sounds like paralysis mode. Let's break that state.

**Physiology:** Stand up right now. 5 deep breaths — slow exhale. Then shake your hands out for 10 seconds. Seriously, do it.

**Focus:** Forget the whole list for a second. What's ONE thing that, if you did it, would make you feel slightly less overwhelmed?

**Language:** "Overwhelmed" means your priorities aren't clear yet. Try: "I have a lot of options — I need to pick the one that matters most right now."

**One action:** Open a blank note. Write down just 3 things. Pick the one that would give you the most relief to have DONE. Do that one for 25 minutes. Nothing else exists.

You don't need to solve everything. You need to start one thing. Motion beats meditation here.

---

## Important Notes

- **Physiology is fastest.** When someone is in a really bad state, start there. Don't try to reason with someone whose body is screaming stress.
- **Keep it short.** This isn't therapy. It's a pattern interrupt and redirect.
- **Don't dismiss the situation.** Validate that they're dealing with something real, then shift.
- **One action matters.** State shifts don't stick unless they're followed by action. Always end with a tiny next step.
- **This is first aid.** If someone is in chronic distress, a state shift helps in the moment but they may need deeper work.
