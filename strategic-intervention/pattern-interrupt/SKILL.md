# Pattern Interrupt

**A pattern interrupt is anything that scrambles the signal so the brain can't keep running the same loop.**

When someone is stuck in rumination, worry, or negative spiraling, they're running a mental program on repeat. Logic rarely breaks these loops — the same thoughts just cycle back. What works: doing something so unexpected that the brain has to reset.

---

## Why Pattern Interrupts Work

The brain is a pattern-completion machine. When a loop starts, it wants to finish the pattern. A good interrupt:
1. Surprises the nervous system
2. Forces attention somewhere else
3. Creates a gap where a new thought can enter

---

## Types of Pattern Interrupts

### Physical Interrupts (Fastest)

The body is the fastest lever. Physical action breaks mental loops.

**Intense physical:**
- 20 jumping jacks
- 10 pushups
- Run up and down stairs
- Shake your entire body hard for 30 seconds
- Cold water on face or wrists
- Hold ice cubes in your hands

**Simple physical:**
- Stand up and move to a different room
- Step outside for 60 seconds
- Stretch: reach arms overhead, twist spine
- Splash cold water on your face
- Change your posture dramatically
- Do 5 deep breaths (4 in, 4 hold, 8 out)

**Radical physical:**
- Cold shower (even 30 seconds)
- Go for a 5-minute walk
- Do a 1-minute plank
- Yell into a pillow

### Mental Interrupts

When physical isn't possible (in a meeting, on a call), use mental interrupts.

**Cognitive challenges:**
- Count backwards from 100 by 7s (100, 93, 86...)
- Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can hear, 3 you can feel (grounding)
- Spell your name backwards, then your mother's name, then your best friend's name
- List cities that start with each letter of the alphabet
- Do mental math: 17 × 13

**Absurd questions:**
- "If I were a penguin, what would I do right now?"
- "What would my 90-year-old self say about this?"
- "What would [someone they admire] do in this exact moment?"

**Perspective shifts:**
- "If this were happening to my best friend, what would I tell them?"
- "How important will this be in 5 years?"
- "What's funny about this situation that I'm not seeing?"

### Verbal/Social Interrupts

**Say something unexpected out loud:**
- "STOP" (said firmly, even if alone)
- A nonsense word ("BANANA!")
- State the obvious opposite: "Everything is perfect and this is easy" (the absurdity breaks the loop)

**Social interrupts:**
- Call someone and talk about literally anything else
- Text someone a question about their day
- Ask someone nearby a random question

### Environment Interrupts

**Change the context:**
- Move to a different room
- Go to a coffee shop
- Work standing instead of sitting
- Change the lighting
- Put on different music (upbeat, or silence if music was playing)
- Open a window

---

## When to Suggest a Pattern Interrupt

**Signs someone needs an interrupt:**
- They're describing the same problem repeatedly with no new insight
- They say things like "I keep thinking about..." or "I can't stop..."
- They're spiraling into worst-case scenarios
- They're cycling between the same few thoughts
- They express feeling "stuck," "trapped," or "going in circles"
- The conversation isn't progressing despite attempts to problem-solve

---

## How to Offer Pattern Interrupts

**Keep it light and direct.** Don't over-explain.

Good:
> "Sounds like you're in a loop. Try this: Stand up right now, shake your body out for 10 seconds, then sit back down. Seriously — do it before you read the next thing I write."

> "That's a spiral. Quick interrupt: count backwards from 100 by 7s until you can't anymore. Then come back."

> "You're going in circles. Physical reset: go splash cold water on your face. I'll wait."

Bad:
> "It seems like you might be experiencing a rumination loop, which happens when the brain gets stuck in repetitive thought patterns. One technique that can help is called a pattern interrupt, which works by..." [Too long, too academic]

---

## Response Format

```
[Brief acknowledgment of the loop]

Quick interrupt: [Specific, concrete action — one sentence]

[Optional: Very brief explanation or follow-up]
```

---

## Example Responses

**User:** "I just keep thinking about what I should have said. I replay the conversation over and over. Why didn't I speak up? I should have said X. I should have done Y. I can't stop thinking about it."

**Response:**
Classic replay loop. Your brain's going to keep running this forever unless you interrupt it.

**Quick interrupt:** Stand up, do 20 jumping jacks, then sit back down. Right now. Before you read on.

The physical reset will scramble the signal. Then we can decide if this actually needs more thought or if it's just a groove your brain is stuck in.

---

**User:** "I'm spiraling about this presentation tomorrow. What if I forget what to say? What if they ask a question I can't answer? What if they think I'm incompetent? I've been anxious about it all day."

**Response:**
That's a worry spiral, and thinking more won't help — your brain's already shown you every worst-case scenario it can generate.

**Interrupt this:** 5-4-3-2-1 grounding. Right now: 5 things you can see, 4 you can hear, 3 you can feel. Say them out loud.

Then: The presentation isn't tomorrow. It's in some number of hours from now, and you have a body of knowledge that got you to this point. What's ONE thing you could do in the next 10 minutes to feel more prepared?

---

**User:** "I don't know what to do about this situation with my friend. I've been going around and around in my head about it. Should I say something? Should I wait? Maybe I'm overreacting? But maybe I'm not? I just keep thinking about it."

**Response:**
You're in a loop — more thinking isn't going to resolve this.

**Interrupt:** Change environments. Get up, go to a different room or step outside for 2 minutes. Move your body. Then come back.

Sometimes the brain just needs a scene change to stop perseverating. Once you're back, we can figure out if this needs action or if it's something to let go.

## Important Notes

- **Physical interrupts work best.** The body is the fastest route to interrupting mental loops.
- **Don't engage the loop first.** If you validate and explore the worry too much, you deepen the groove. Interrupt first, then (if needed) process.
- **They need to actually do it.** Ask them to do the interrupt before reading on. If they just read the suggestion and keep typing, the interrupt won't work.
- **This isn't dismissal.** Some problems need real processing. But processing only works from a clearer state. Interrupt first, think second.
- **Chronic loops may need more.** If someone is stuck in the same loop across multiple conversations, they may need deeper work (Dickens, NAC, or professional support).
