# State-Story-Strategy Diagnostic

When someone says "I'm stuck," they're stuck at one of three levels. Applying the wrong fix wastes time and energy.

**The Three Levels:**

1. **State** — Your emotional and physiological condition right now. Energy, mood, physical state. When you're in a weak state, even good strategies feel impossible.

2. **Story** — The meaning you're assigning to the situation. The beliefs, narratives, and interpretations running in the background. "I'm not good enough." "This always happens to me." "It's too late."

3. **Strategy** — The actual tactics, information, and action steps. The "how to."

**The Critical Insight:**
Most people think they have a Strategy problem when they actually have a State or Story problem. They keep searching for better tactics, more information, another course — when the real issue is they're exhausted (State) or they believe they'll fail anyway (Story).

---

## The Diagnostic Process

### Step 1: Ask the revealing question

> "Do you know what you need to do, or are you genuinely unsure of the next step?"

- If they **know what to do but aren't doing it** → It's NOT a Strategy problem. It's State or Story.
- If they **genuinely don't know the next step** → It might be Strategy, but probe deeper.

### Step 2: Check State first

State problems have physical and emotional markers:

**Physical signs:**
- Exhaustion, poor sleep
- Hunger, dehydration
- Physical tension, shallow breathing
- Illness or pain

**Emotional signs:**
- Overwhelm
- Anxiety or fear
- Apathy, numbness
- Irritability

**Key questions:**
- "When did you last feel energized about this?"
- "How's your sleep/eating/exercise been?"
- "What's your body telling you right now?"
- "If you had twice the energy, would you know what to do?"

If they're in a weak physical or emotional state, that's the bottleneck. No amount of strategy or mindset work will help until State is addressed.

### Step 3: Check Story second

Story problems reveal themselves through language patterns:

**Identity statements:**
- "I'm just not a [type of person]"
- "I've never been good at..."
- "People like me don't..."

**Limiting beliefs:**
- "It's too late"
- "I'll just fail again"
- "It won't work because..."
- "I don't deserve..."

**Meaning assignments:**
- "This always happens to me"
- "See, I told you so"
- "This proves that..."

**Key questions:**
- "What do you believe about yourself in this situation?"
- "What are you afraid this will mean about you if you try and fail?"
- "What story are you telling yourself about why this won't work?"
- "If you knew you couldn't fail, what would you do?"

If they light up at "if you couldn't fail" but go dark when reality returns — it's Story.

### Step 4: Confirm Strategy (only if State and Story check out)

True Strategy problems are rare. They look like:

- Genuine lack of information ("I literally don't know how to set up an LLC")
- Missing a specific skill ("I don't know how to code")
- Need for a specific tactic ("What's the best way to reach out to investors?")

**Key sign of a real Strategy problem:** They have energy, they believe they can succeed, they just need the "how."

If someone says "I need a strategy" but they're also exhausted and believe they'll fail — Strategy isn't the real problem.

## Example

**User:** "I know I need to start posting on LinkedIn to grow my business. I've written a content strategy. I have 10 posts drafted. But every time I go to hit publish, I just... don't. I keep telling myself I'll do it tomorrow. It's been three weeks."

**Diagnostic:**

### Primary Blockage: STORY

### Evidence
- They know what to do (Strategy is present)
- They have energy to draft 10 posts (State seems adequate)
- The freeze happens at the moment of publishing = fear of judgment
- "I'll do it tomorrow" = avoidance pattern, not exhaustion

### The Story Running in the Background
"If I put myself out there and it doesn't work, it proves I'm not good enough. Better to stay in preparation mode where I can't fail."

### The Prescription
This isn't about content strategy. It's about the meaning they're attaching to visibility.

**The belief to address:** "Publishing and getting no response = I'm not valuable"

**Reframe:** Publishing is data collection, not a verdict on your worth. A post that flops teaches you what doesn't resonate. A post that works teaches you what does. Both are wins. The only failure is never learning what your audience responds to.

**The intervention:** Commit to posting 10 pieces in 10 days with zero attachment to metrics. The goal isn't engagement — it's breaking the belief that your value depends on likes.

### The One Thing to Do First
Post the first draft right now, before this conversation ends. Don't read the comments for 48 hours.

---

## Important Notes

- Most "Strategy" problems are Story problems in disguise. Probe before accepting the frame.
- State problems must be addressed first. A depleted person cannot do good mindset work.
- Story problems often present as "I need more information" — endless research is avoidance.
- When in doubt, ask: "If you felt amazing and knew you couldn't fail, would you know what to do?" The answer usually reveals the real blocker.
