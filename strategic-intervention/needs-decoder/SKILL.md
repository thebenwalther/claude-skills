# Needs Decoder

Diagnoses which of the Six Human Needs are driving a behavior, decision, or internal conflict.

Most people don't understand why they do what they do. They think they're lazy, broken, or lack willpower. The truth: every behavior is an attempt to meet one or more of the Six Human Needs. The question isn't "why can't I stop?" — it's "what need is this meeting that I haven't found another way to meet?"

---

## The Six Human Needs

### The Four Needs of the Personality

**1. Certainty**
The need for safety, stability, predictability, and comfort. Knowing what's coming. Feeling in control.

- Constructive: Building systems, saving money, developing expertise, healthy routines
- Destructive: Controlling others, refusing to take risks, staying in dead-end situations because they're familiar, addiction to comfort

**2. Variety (Uncertainty)**
The need for change, surprise, challenge, novelty, and adventure. The opposite of certainty — and we need both.

- Constructive: Learning new skills, travel, creative projects, physical challenges
- Destructive: Drama creation, affairs, impulsive decisions, substance use for stimulation, chaos addiction

**3. Significance**
The need to feel important, special, needed, unique, or worthy of attention. Mattering.

- Constructive: Achieving excellence, developing rare skills, contributing uniquely, earned recognition
- Destructive: Tearing others down, bragging, victim identity, collecting problems, superiority/inferiority complexes

**4. Love & Connection**
The need for closeness, intimacy, belonging, and feeling bonded to others. Many settle for connection (being around people) when they can't get love.

- Constructive: Deep relationships, vulnerability, community, presence with others
- Destructive: People-pleasing, codependency, staying in toxic relationships, sacrificing self for approval

### The Two Needs of the Spirit

**5. Growth**
The need to expand, develop, become more, progress. Stagnation feels like death.

- Constructive: Learning, skill development, self-improvement, pushing edges
- Destructive: Never finishing anything (always onto the next growth opportunity), perfectionism as disguised growth obsession

**6. Contribution**
The need to give, serve, and make a difference beyond yourself. The need that makes the other five sustainable.

- Constructive: Service, mentoring, creating value for others, legacy work
- Destructive: Martyrdom, giving to feel significant, helping to avoid own issues

## How to Run a Needs Decoder

### Step 1: Identify the behavior or pattern

Ask the user to describe the specific behavior, decision, or conflict. Get concrete:
- What exactly are you doing (or not doing)?
- How often? In what contexts?
- What happens right before? Right after?

### Step 2: Map the needs being met

For the behavior in question, identify which needs it's currently meeting. Most behaviors meet at least 2-3 needs — that's why they're so hard to change.

Ask yourself (and explore with the user):
- **Certainty**: Does this behavior make them feel safe, comfortable, or in control?
- **Variety**: Does it provide excitement, novelty, or escape from boredom?
- **Significance**: Does it make them feel important, special, or noticed?
- **Connection**: Does it create a sense of belonging or closeness (even negative attention is connection)?
- **Growth**: Does it feel like progress or learning, even if illusory?
- **Contribution**: Does it involve giving to others (even if unhealthy giving)?

### Step 3: Rate the vehicle

Rate how the current behavior meets each need on a scale of 1-10. A behavior that meets 3+ needs at a 7+ level is extremely hard to change without a replacement.

### Step 4: Identify the conflict (if applicable)

If the user is feeling torn or stuck, identify which needs are in conflict:
- Certainty vs. Variety (safety vs. adventure)
- Significance vs. Connection (standing out vs. belonging)
- Growth vs. Certainty (risk vs. comfort)

### Step 5: Find constructive alternatives

For each need being met destructively, brainstorm constructive alternatives that could meet the same need at a similar level. The goal isn't to eliminate the need — it's to find a healthier vehicle for meeting it.

## Output Format

Present the diagnosis clearly:

```
## Needs Decoder: [Behavior/Pattern Name]

### What You're Doing
[Concrete description of the behavior]

### Needs Being Met
| Need | Level (1-10) | How It's Being Met |
|------|--------------|-------------------|
| [Need] | [Score] | [Explanation] |

### The Core Conflict (if applicable)
[Which needs are pulling in opposite directions and why]

### Why This Is Hard to Change
[Explain how many needs this meets and at what level]

### Constructive Alternatives
For [Need 1]: [Alternative that could meet it at a similar level]
For [Need 2]: [Alternative]
...

### The Pattern Interrupt
[One specific thing they could do differently to start shifting the pattern]
```

## Example

**User:** "I keep starting businesses and abandoning them right when they start working. I've done this 4 times. I don't understand why I can't just stick with something."

**Needs Decoder:**

| Need | Level | How It's Being Met |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| Variety | 9/10 | New business = new challenge, new learning, new excitement |
| Significance | 8/10 | "I'm a serial entrepreneur" identity, starting is more impressive than grinding |
| Growth | 9/10 | Steep learning curve at the start, feels like massive progress |
| Certainty | 2/10 | Actually undermined — no stable income, constant reinvention |

**The conflict:** Growth/Variety vs. Certainty. The excitement of starting meets three needs at very high levels. But success requires the boring middle — which only meets Certainty (which you're not currently valuing).

**Why it's hard:** This pattern meets 3 needs at 8+ levels. You're not broken or afraid of success. You're addicted to the startup dopamine because it's the most efficient vehicle you've found for Variety, Significance, and Growth simultaneously.

**Constructive alternative:** You need a way to get Variety, Significance, and Growth *within* a single business. Options:
- Launch new product lines instead of new companies
- Set 90-day "seasons" with completely different focuses
- Build a personal brand around the journey (Significance from the process, not just the launch)

**Pattern interrupt:** Before abandoning the current venture, identify what need is screaming and find a way to meet it *inside* this business.

---

## Important Notes

- Never tell someone their need is wrong. All six needs are legitimate. The only question is how they're being met.
- Destructive patterns often meet multiple needs at high levels. That's why they're so sticky.
- The goal isn't to eliminate a need — you can't. The goal is to find a better vehicle.
- When needs conflict, the solution is usually to find a way to meet both, not to pick one.
