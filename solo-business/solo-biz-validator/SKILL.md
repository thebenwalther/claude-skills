# Solo Biz Validator

Most business ideas die not because the product is bad — but because nobody validated demand before building. The graveyard of solo businesses is full of beautifully-built products that solved problems nobody had (or nobody would pay to fix).

This skill runs two frameworks back-to-back:

1. **P.L.A.N. Framework** — Deconstructs your target market's psyche to find real, paying problems
2. **Flight Test Sequence** — Moves from research to real-world financial commitment

The goal: prove (or kill) a business idea with evidence, not feelings.

---

## Phase 1: The P.L.A.N. Framework

P.L.A.N. stands for **Problems, Language, Anecdotes, Needs**. It's a structured method for getting inside the head of your target customer before you build anything.

### P — Problems

The specific pain points your audience is dealing with right now. Not what you *think* they struggle with — what they're actually complaining about in the wild.

**How to find them:**
- Reddit threads (sort by top/controversial in relevant subreddits)
- Facebook group discussions
- Negative reviews of competing products
- Quora questions in the niche
- YouTube comment sections on competitor content
- X/Twitter complaints and rants

**What you're looking for:**
- Recurring frustrations (same complaint from multiple people)
- Problems people have tried and failed to solve
- Problems people are willing to spend money on (not just annoyances)
- Emotional intensity — the more frustrated, the more they'll pay

**Red flags:**
- Only one person mentions the problem (not widespread)
- People complain but would never pay to fix it
- The problem is too vague to build a product around
- Existing solutions already solve it well and cheaply

**Ask:** "What are the top 3-5 problems that come up again and again in the places your audience gathers?"

### L — Language

The exact words and phrasing your audience uses to describe their struggles. This is gold for marketing copy — when you use their language, they feel instantly understood.

**Capture verbatim quotes like:**
- "I'm so tired of..."
- "Why can't someone just..."
- "I've tried everything but..."
- "The thing that frustrates me most is..."

**Why this matters:**
- Using their exact words in your copy creates instant rapport
- Industry jargon tells you their sophistication level
- Emotional language reveals intensity of the problem
- Phrasing reveals what they've already tried (and what hasn't worked)

**Ask:** "What are the exact phrases your target audience uses when describing this problem? Copy-paste real quotes."

### A — Anecdotes

Short, relatable stories that illustrate the context around the problem. These become the raw material for your marketing — blog posts, email stories, social proof, and "me too" moments.

**What to capture:**
- Specific situations where the problem shows up
- Before/after stories from people who've solved it
- Failures and frustrations (these resonate more than success stories initially)
- The emotional journey — what it feels like to be stuck

**Ask:** "Can you share a specific story — yours or someone else's — that captures what this problem looks like in real life?"

### N — Needs

The fundamental requirements the audience believes will solve their problems. This is where you bridge from "problem" to "product."

**Map needs to potential deliverables:**

| Stated Need | Possible Deliverable |
|-------------|---------------------|
| "I need a step-by-step plan" | Roadmap / Course |
| "I just need someone to tell me what to do" | Coaching / Consulting |
| "I need templates I can copy" | Template Pack / Swipe Files |
| "I need to see how someone else did it" | Case Study / Behind-the-Scenes |
| "I need accountability" | Community / Group Program |
| "I need it done for me" | Done-for-You Service |

**Ask:** "When your target audience imagines their problem solved, what do they think the solution looks like?"

## Phase 2: The Flight Test Sequence

Research is not validation. Only money is validation. The Flight Test moves from "people say they want this" to "people will pay for this."

### Step 1: Narrow the Problem

Take the top-ranked problem from P.L.A.N. and distill it to its sharpest form:

- **Too broad:** "People struggle with marketing"
- **Narrowed:** "Solo consultants can't consistently generate leads without cold outreach"
- **Sharp:** "Solo consultants waste 10+ hours/week on lead gen that produces zero qualified prospects"

**Test:** Does this problem make someone wince and nod? If not, sharpen it.

### Step 2: The One-Sentence Challenge

Explain the solution in one sentence. If you can't, the idea is too complex.

**Formula:** "I help [specific person] [achieve specific outcome] without [biggest pain/obstacle]."

**Examples:**
- "I help freelance designers land $5K+ clients without cold pitching or competing on price."
- "I help new solopreneurs build their first digital product in a weekend using plug-and-play templates."

**If you can't write this sentence clearly, go back to Step 1.** Complexity is a symptom of unclear thinking, not a sign of sophistication.

### Step 3: Hyper-Target an Audience

Identify 10-100 people who fit your ideal customer profile. These are real humans, not demographics:

**Where to find them:**
- Your existing network (LinkedIn, email contacts)
- Niche communities you're already part of
- People who've engaged with similar content
- Respondents to a simple poll or survey

**What makes a good test group:**
- They have the problem (not just "interested in the topic")
- They have the ability to pay
- They're accessible (you can actually reach them)

### Step 4: The Pre-Sale

The only validation that counts. Ask for money before the product exists.

**Pre-sale approaches (ranked by validation strength):**

1. **Direct ask:** "I'm building [solution]. It'll cost $X. Want in?" — Strongest signal
2. **Waitlist with deposit:** "Put down $25 to reserve your spot" — Strong signal
3. **Founding member offer:** "First 10 get it at 50% off. Pay now, get it when it launches" — Strong signal
4. **Waitlist (free):** "Sign up to be notified" — Weak signal (interest ≠ payment)
5. **Survey/feedback:** "Would you buy this?" — Almost worthless

**Pre-sale script template:**

> "Hey [Name], I'm building [one-sentence solution]. It's for [specific person] who struggles with [specific problem].
>
> Here's what it includes: [2-3 bullet deliverables]
>
> I'm offering founding member pricing at $[price] for the first [number] people. You'd get [bonus for early buyers].
>
> Would you be interested?"

**Interpreting results:**
- 3+ sales from 20 conversations = strong signal, build it
- 1-2 sales from 20 conversations = weak signal, refine the offer or pivot
- 0 sales from 20 conversations = either wrong audience, wrong problem, or wrong price — diagnose which

### Step 5: Post-Mortem

Whether the pre-sale succeeds or fails, extract the data:

**If it worked:**
- What objections came up? (These inform your sales page)
- What resonated most? (Double down on this in marketing)
- What questions did people ask? (These become FAQ and content)

**If it failed:**
- Was it the problem? (Did people recognize the pain?)
- Was it the solution? (Did the deliverable feel right?)
- Was it the price? (Would they buy at a different price?)
- Was it the audience? (Were these the right people?)

## Important Notes

- **"Interesting" is not validation.** People saying "cool idea!" means nothing. Money talks.
- **Pre-sell before you build.** Building first is the #1 mistake of solo entrepreneurs.
- **Small sample, big signal.** 20 real conversations beat 1,000 survey responses.
- **Failure is data, not defeat.** A killed idea saves months of wasted effort.
- **Speed beats perfection.** The entire validation process should take 1-2 weeks, not months.
- **The niche matters more than the product.** A mediocre product for the right audience beats a brilliant product for the wrong one.
