# Niche Clarity Engine

Most people pick a niche based on what seems profitable. Then they burn out because the work drains them, or they pivot every 3 months because nothing sticks. The fix isn't more market research — it's internal alignment first.

This skill runs three diagnostic lenses in sequence, then synthesizes them into a niche recommendation:

1. **Airport Test** — What do you want your life to look like? (Future pull)
2. **History Test** — Where have you been most alive and effective? (Past evidence)
3. **Anti-Vision** — What do you refuse to tolerate? (Negative push)

The intersection of all three is where your niche lives.

---

## Lens 1: The Airport Test (Future Vision)

Imagine you're at an airport five years from now. You run into an old friend who asks, "How's life?" You light up and describe your ideal reality.

### The Exercise

Guide the user through these prompts — and push for specifics, not platitudes:

**Daily life:**
- "Walk me through your ideal Tuesday. What does the morning look like? Where are you?"
- "What kind of work are you doing between 9am and noon?"
- "Who are you working with — or are you alone?"
- "What does your afternoon look like?"

**Work structure:**
- "How many hours per week are you working?"
- "Are you creating content, building products, coaching people, managing a team — what?"
- "Do you have employees? Contractors? Or is it just you?"
- "How do people find you — do they come to you, or do you go to them?"

**Money and freedom:**
- "How much are you earning? Be specific."
- "Is the income consistent, or does it fluctuate?"
- "What does financial security look like to you — a number, a feeling, or both?"
- "What have you been able to say 'yes' to that you can't right now?"

**Identity and meaning:**
- "What are you known for?"
- "What would your friend say when they describe your work to someone else?"
- "What's the most meaningful project you've completed in the last year?"

### What This Reveals

The Airport Test isn't about business strategy — it's about lifestyle architecture. The answers dictate which business models are even viable:

| If they want... | Then they should avoid... | And lean toward... |
|----------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Maximum time freedom | Client-heavy services | Digital products, automation |
| Deep relationships | Faceless content businesses | Coaching, community |
| Location independence | Local service businesses | Online-first models |
| Creative expression | Template/commodity work | Content creation, category design |
| High income, fewer clients | Low-ticket products | High-ticket services, consulting |
| Total autonomy | Partnerships, teams | Solo operation with contractors |

## Lens 2: The History Test (Past Pattern Recognition)

The best predictor of where you'll thrive is where you've already thrived. This isn't about your resume — it's about energy patterns.

### The Exercise

Create a chronological map of every significant role, project, hobby, and experience:

**For each entry, capture:**
1. What were you doing?
2. What specific tasks energized you most?
3. What tasks drained you?
4. What results did you produce? (Even small wins count)
5. What did people come to you for? (Your informal reputation)
6. On a scale of 1-10, how alive did you feel?

**Then look for patterns:**

- **Recurring themes:** What shows up across multiple entries? (Teaching, building systems, writing, problem-solving, leading)
- **Energy spikes:** Where were you rated 8+ on the "alive" scale? What was common?
- **Natural authority:** What did people consistently ask you for help with?
- **Unfair advantages:** What combinations of skills/experience would be hard for someone else to replicate?

### The Personal SWOT

After the history mapping, run a quick SWOT:

**Strengths:** Skills and traits that come naturally and produce results
**Weaknesses:** Areas that consistently drain energy or produce poor results
**Opportunities:** Market gaps that align with your strengths
**Threats:** External factors that could undermine the niche (saturation, AI disruption, platform risk)

**Critical question for SWOT:** "What do people in your life — colleagues, friends, family — say you're unusually good at? Not what you think — what they tell you."

### What This Reveals

The History Test produces:
- A list of **core competencies** (what you're built for)
- Your **unfair advantage** (the unique intersection nobody else has)
- **Energy filters** (what to pursue vs. avoid based on evidence, not theory)

## Lens 3: The Anti-Vision (What You Refuse to Tolerate)

Dan Koe's Anti-Vision creates a psychological "electric fence" around the life you're building away from. Most goal-setting focuses on what you want. The Anti-Vision focuses on what you refuse to accept — which is often more motivating.

### The Exercise

**Describe in vivid detail the life you're actively running from:**

- "If nothing changes and you stay on your current default path, what does your life look like in 5 years?"
- "What's the worst version of your work life that you've already experienced?"
- "What daily activities make you feel like you're wasting your life?"
- "What kind of boss, client, or colleague do you never want to deal with again?"
- "What financial situation would feel like failure to you?"

**Then identify the non-negotiables:**

- "I will never again [specific thing]."
- "I refuse to build a business that requires [specific constraint]."
- "I will not trade [specific value] for money."

### What This Reveals

The Anti-Vision produces hard constraints — boundaries that filter out entire niche categories:

| Anti-Vision Statement | Niche Filter |
|----------------------|-------------|
| "I refuse to trade hours for dollars" | Eliminates pure services without leverage |
| "I will never manage employees again" | Keeps it solo or contractor-based |
| "I won't do work that doesn't let me be creative" | Eliminates template/commodity niches |
| "I refuse to be on camera" | Favors written content, faceless models |
| "I will never answer to a boss again" | Eliminates consulting for one big client |
| "I won't build something that chains me to a desk" | Requires mobile-first, async work |

## The Synthesis: Finding the Intersection

After running all three lenses, overlay the results:

### Step 1: Extract Core Data

From each lens, pull:
- **Airport Test:** Desired lifestyle + business model constraints
- **History Test:** Core competencies + unfair advantage + energy patterns
- **Anti-Vision:** Hard constraints + non-negotiables

### Step 2: Find the Overlap

The niche lives at the intersection of:
- **What you want your life to look like** (Airport Test)
- **What you're naturally built to do** (History Test)
- **What you refuse to tolerate** (Anti-Vision)

Draw the Venn diagram mentally. Where all three circles overlap = your niche territory.

### Step 3: Market-Test the Territory

Apply two filters:
1. **Does this niche fall within one of the four eternal markets?** (Health, Wealth, Relationships, Happiness) — If yes, there's always demand.
2. **Are people already paying for solutions in this space?** — Competition is validation, not a threat.

### Step 4: Craft the Niche Statement

**Formula:** "I help [specific person] achieve [specific outcome] using [my unique approach/advantage]."

The niche statement should pass three tests:
- **Airport Test:** "Would this business give me the life I described?"
- **History Test:** "Am I built for this work? Do I have evidence?"
- **Anti-Vision Test:** "Does this violate any of my non-negotiables?"

If it passes all three — you've found it.

## Important Notes

- **Internal alignment comes first.** A profitable niche you hate is worse than a less profitable one you love. Burnout kills more businesses than bad strategy.
- **The unfair advantage is the moat.** Skills can be learned. Experience can be gained. But the specific intersection of YOUR history, personality, and perspective is unreplicable.
- **The Anti-Vision is not optional.** Skipping this step is how people end up building businesses they eventually resent.
- **Niche ≠ prison.** A niche is a starting point. Once you establish authority, you can expand. But you can't start broad and narrow down — that's how you stay invisible.
- **If all three lenses don't align, don't force it.** Go back and get honest about what you actually want, not what sounds impressive.
- **This pairs naturally with the Solo Biz Validator.** After finding clarity here, validate demand with the P.L.A.N. framework.
