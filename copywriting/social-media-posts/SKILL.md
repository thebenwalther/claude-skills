# Social Media Post Creator — LinkedIn & X

This skill writes platform-optimized posts that sound like a sharp, seasoned human wrote them — not a content scheduler. Every post is built to stop the scroll, spark replies, and serve a strategic purpose (research, authority-building, or audience growth).

---

## Before Writing Anything

Ask these questions if not already answered in context:

1. **Platform**: LinkedIn, X, or both?
2. **Goal**: Research (gather replies/data), authority (position as an expert), or growth (reach new people)?
3. **Topic or idea**: What's the core message, insight, or question?
4. **Voice**: Does the user have an established tone? Check conversation history first.
5. **Format**: Does the user want options, or one strong version?

If the context already contains this info — skip asking and write.

## Platform Rules (Data-Backed)

### X (Twitter)
- **Best format**: Text-only. Median engagement rate 3.56% — leads all formats.
- **Length**: 70–100 characters for quick reads. Threads work at 4–8 posts each.
- **Structure**: One idea. Hook line. White space. Question or provocation at the end.
- **No hashtags**: They signal beginner and don't meaningfully help the algorithm.
- **No links in the post**: Kills reach. Put links in replies if needed.
- **Engagement window**: Reply to comments fast — first hour matters most.
- **Voice**: Raw, direct, fast. Hot takes. Pattern interrupts. Punchy one-liners.

### LinkedIn
- **Best formats by goal**:
  - *Reach/growth*: Text + personal photo (not stock, not graphics). Carousels when you have the production capacity.
  - *Authority/depth*: Carousel (PDF slides) — 24.42% avg engagement rate, 3.7x over text-only.
  - *Research/replies*: Text-only with strong question at the end.
- **Hook rule**: First 140 characters show before "See more" on desktop, 110 on mobile. The hook IS the post — if it doesn't land, nothing else matters.
- **Structure**: Short paragraphs. 2–3 sentences max. Line breaks between every paragraph. Never a wall of text.
- **No links in the post body**: LinkedIn penalizes external links. Put them in the first comment.
- **Faces get 38% more likes**: Recommend a personal photo whenever possible.
- **Early engagement matters**: First-hour replies signal to the algorithm.
- **Voice**: More narrative than X. Credibility-forward. Stories land. But never corporate, never hollow.

## The Red Flag Test

Run every post through this before delivering it. Would a seasoned, real human be embarrassed to post this? Watch for:

- ❌ "I'm excited to share..." — hollow opener, delete immediately
- ❌ "Thoughts?" as the only CTA — lazy, vague, doesn't earn replies
- ❌ Perfect symmetry in bullet lists — signals AI, not a person
- ❌ Motivational poster language — "Success is a journey, not a destination"
- ❌ Hedging — "I think," "it seems," "perhaps"
- ❌ Corporate speak — "leverage," "utilize," "synergy," "game-changer"
- ❌ Hashtag spam — #productivity #growth #mindset #entrepreneur
- ❌ Wall of text with no breathing room
- ❌ Ending on a statement instead of an invitation

If any of these appear — rewrite before delivering.

## Post Structures That Work

### X — Core Formats

**The Pattern Interrupt**
```
[Counterintuitive statement.]

[1–2 lines of unpacking — why the conventional take is wrong.]

[Specific observation or evidence.]

[Question that invites a reply.]
```

**The Hot Take**
```
Unpopular opinion:

[Bold claim in one line.]

[Brief reason or nuance.]

Agree or disagree?
```

**The Observation**
```
[Specific thing you keep noticing — name it precisely.]

[Why it matters or what it reveals.]

[Invite others to share theirs.]
```

---

### LinkedIn — Core Formats

**The Story Hook (Text-only or Text + Photo)**
```
[Single provocative or specific opening line — no "I" as the first word if possible.]

[2–3 lines of context or story. Keep it human and specific.]

[The insight or pattern you've identified.]

[Invitation to share — make it easy to reply with a specific question.]
```

**The List Post (Text-only)**
```
[Bold claim or hook line.]

[Setup: 1–2 lines of framing.]

[List — short, punchy, each item on its own line with white space]

→ Item one
→ Item two
→ Item three

[Closing line that reframes or adds weight.]

[Question.]
```

**The Observation + Question (Research Post)**
```
[Name a pattern you keep seeing — specific, not vague.]

[1–2 lines of why it matters or what it signals.]

[What you've noticed about it personally — makes it credible.]

[Direct question that's easy to answer in one sentence.]
```

## Carousel Structure (LinkedIn — when requested)

Carousels are the highest-engagement format on LinkedIn (24.42% avg). Use when the user wants to teach something, share a framework, or break down a process.

**Slide structure:**
- **Slide 1 (Cover)**: Bold hook. Promise a specific outcome. Single image or clean graphic.
- **Slides 2–8**: One idea per slide. Big text. Minimal copy. Visual hierarchy.
- **Last slide**: Summary + soft CTA ("Save this" or "What would you add?")

**Caption formula:**
```
[Hook — mirrors the cover slide promise]

[1–2 lines of framing]

Swipe to see all [X] → 

[Soft CTA at the end]
```

## Voice Calibration

Before writing, check conversation history for the user's tone, background, and niche. Then calibrate:

| If they are... | Write like... |
|----------------|---------------|
| Early-stage solopreneur | Honest, building-in-public, slightly vulnerable but confident |
| Technical/systems thinker | Sharp observations, frameworks, specific over general |
| Coach or educator | Story-driven, outcome-focused, reader-first |
| Executive or consultant | Authoritative but not corporate, insight-dense |

**Universal rules regardless of niche:**
- Active voice. Short sentences. No hedging.
- Contractions are fine — "you're," "it's," "don't"
- One clear idea per post — don't try to say three things at once
- End with an invitation, not a conclusion

---

## Output Format

Always deliver:

1. **The post** — ready to copy-paste, formatted exactly as it would appear on the platform (line breaks, spacing, structure)
2. **Platform note** — one line on why the format was chosen
3. **Optional photo prompt** — if a personal photo would help (LinkedIn), suggest what kind
4. **Variation** — if the goal is research/testing, offer a second angle

If writing for both platforms in one request — write them as distinct pieces, not the same post reformatted. X and LinkedIn have different voices, lengths, and purposes.
