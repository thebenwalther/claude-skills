# Hook Library — Opening Lines That Stop the Scroll

A hook's only job: make the next line impossible not to read.
The first line is not the intro. It's the headline. Treat it that way.

---

## Rules for Hooks

- Never start with "I" (LinkedIn) — it centers you instead of the reader
- Never start with "In today's..." or "In the world of..."
- Never open with a question that can be answered with "no" — they'll move on
- Avoid "Let's talk about..." — weak, filler, earns nothing
- Specific beats vague every time: "15 years" beats "years of experience"
- Tension beats insight: readers lean in when something is unsettled

---

## Hook Structures by Type

### The Counterintuitive Statement
Challenge what most people believe. Creates cognitive dissonance — they need to resolve it.

```
Most [common advice] is wrong.
The [popular thing] everyone recommends is actually what's keeping you stuck.
You don't have a [X] problem. You have a [Y] problem.
[Conventional wisdom] sounds smart. It's usually the trap.
Stop [widely-recommended action]. Here's what actually works.
```

**Examples:**
- "You don't have a productivity problem. You have a self-knowledge problem dressed up as a productivity problem."
- "The second brain everyone's building isn't the problem. It's that they built someone else's."
- "Most ambitious people aren't failing because they lack discipline. They're running the wrong operating system."

---

### The Specific Observation
Name something you keep seeing. Specificity signals credibility — only someone who's actually in it notices this.

```
Something I keep noticing:
A pattern I can't stop seeing:
After [X years / X conversations / X clients]:
The most [adjective] people I know all [specific behavior].
Every [person type] I've talked to says the same thing:
```

**Examples:**
- "Something I keep noticing: the most stuck people aren't lazy. They're the hardest workers in the room."
- "After 15 years building systems for other people, here's what nobody tells you about building one for yourself."
- "Every ambitious professional I talk to has the same problem. They know exactly what to do. They just can't make themselves do it."

---

### The Uncomfortable Truth
State something true that people know but don't say out loud. Validates their private experience.

```
Nobody talks about this, but [uncomfortable truth].
Here's what [authority figures / gurus / courses] don't tell you:
The thing everyone's too polite to say about [topic]:
[Widely celebrated thing] has a dark side nobody mentions.
```

**Examples:**
- "Nobody talks about this, but most productivity systems are designed for someone else's life."
- "Here's what the 'building a second brain' crowd doesn't tell you: the system is the easy part."

---

### The Number Hook
Numbers create specificity and imply a contained, digestible insight.

```
[X] things I wish I knew before [doing thing]:
After [X] years / conversations / attempts:
[X]% of [people] struggle with [thing]. Here's why.
In [X] years of [doing thing], I've seen one pattern repeat:
```

**Examples:**
- "3 signs your productivity system was built for someone else's life"
- "After 15 years in education and IT, I've seen one thing kill more potential than anything else"

---

### The Question Hook (Use Sparingly)
Only works when the question creates genuine tension — not a yes/no, not rhetorical.

```
What would you do if [scenario that challenges assumptions]?
Why do [smart/ambitious people] keep [doing thing that doesn't work]?
When was the last time you [specific reflective action]?
```

**Bad:** "Have you ever struggled with productivity?"
**Good:** "What does your operating system actually look like — and did you build it, or did you inherit it?"

---

### The Story Hook
Drop directly into a specific moment. No setup, no context — just the scene.

```
[Age / year / specific moment], I [did thing that led to insight].
A [client / friend / colleague] said something last week that I can't stop thinking about:
[Person] asked me [specific question]. I didn't have a good answer.
Three months ago, [specific thing happened]. Here's what it revealed.
```

**Examples:**
- "A client said something last week I can't stop thinking about: 'I have every tool. I still can't move.'"
- "Six months ago I built a system to close the gap between who I was and who I was trying to become. Here's what I learned."

---

### The Provocative Take (X-optimized)
Short. Punchy. Designed to create a reaction — agreement or disagreement both work.

```
[Bold one-line claim.]
Unpopular opinion: [claim]
Hot take: [claim]
[Common belief]. [Short rebuttal.]
```

**Examples:**
- "Unpopular opinion: discipline is a symptom, not a solution."
- "Your second brain is full. Your first brain still runs the show."
- "Tools don't transform people. Identity does."

---

## Hooks to Avoid (Red Flag List)

| Weak Hook | Why It Fails | Fix |
|-----------|-------------|-----|
| "I'm excited to share..." | Centers the writer, not the reader | Start with the insight |
| "In today's fast-paced world..." | Generic, zero specificity | Name the actual thing |
| "Let's talk about [topic]." | Weak invitation, earns nothing | Make a claim instead |
| "Have you ever...?" | Too easy to answer no | Create tension, not a question |
| "I've been thinking a lot about..." | Vague, no payoff promised | State what you concluded |
| "This might be controversial but..." | Hedging before the take | Just say the thing |
| "Grateful for..." | Performance, not insight | Earn the gratitude with a lesson |
