# Copy Framework Router

Routes any copywriting request to the right framework from five digital authority masters — then drafts the output with masters-level composition baked in.

---

## Step 1: Gather Inputs

Determine these three things before selecting a framework. If any are missing or ambiguous, ask once — briefly — before proceeding.

1. **Goal** — What is this piece trying to do?
   - Build awareness / establish authority
   - Drive newsletter signups or follows
   - Convert to a sale or offer
   - Deliver standalone value / educate
   - Spark engagement (replies, reshares)

2. **Platform** — Where will this live?
   - LinkedIn
   - X (Twitter)
   - Newsletter / email
   - Landing page / sales page

3. **Topic** — The core idea, story, or message. Ask the user to be specific. "Productivity" is not a topic. "Why most people's morning routines fail by 9am" is.

---

## Step 2: Select the Framework

Use this routing table. When inputs map to multiple frameworks, select the best fit and briefly note the alternative.

| Goal | Platform | Primary Framework | Secondary |
|------|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Convert / sell | Any | PAIPS | 5-4-3-2-1 |
| Awareness / authority | LinkedIn, X | VFA | PAIPS |
| Engagement / narrative depth | X, Newsletter | Pain-Insight-Action | VFA |
| Standalone value, no CTA | LinkedIn, X | Zero-Click Micro-Story | 4A layering |
| Short persuasive post | Any | BPAS | Pyramid Principle |
| Long-form newsletter | Newsletter | Pain-Insight-Action | PAIPS |
| Sales / landing page | Landing page | 5-4-3-2-1 | PAIPS |

## Step 3: Apply the Framework

### PAIPS (Justin Welsh)
**Best for:** Conversion-focused posts, newsletter growth, LinkedIn authority

1. **Problem** — Identify a visceral, specific pain point. Make it felt, not stated.
2. **Agitate** — Hammer the consequences. "Turn the knife." Don't move on until the reader feels the problem personally and urgently. This is the power step most writers skip.
3. **Intrigue** — Introduce a pattern break — a counter-intuitive idea or reframe that pivots the reader from distress to curiosity.
4. **Positive Future** — Paint the desired state after the problem is solved. Make it concrete and emotionally real, not abstract.
5. **Solution** — Present the specific offer, insight, or CTA.

*Craft note:* The opening line must be visceral — not "Many people struggle with X." Lead with the wound. Agitation is what separates PAIPS from generic PAS. Don't rush it.

---

### VFA (Justin Welsh)
**Best for:** Short authority-building posts, tweets, engaging LinkedIn content

1. **Visceral Opener** — Dramatic, sensory language. Don't describe what happened — make the reader feel it. "I escaped the rat race" not "I left my job."
2. **Fresh Perspective** — A counter-intuitive insight or reframe. The cognitive relief or hope that shifts how the reader sees the problem.
3. **Anaphora** — Repeat a word or phrase at the start of 3–5 successive sentences to build rhythmic momentum. Like a boxer throwing jabs. Each one hits harder than the last.

*Craft note:* The opener must replace passive description with active imagery. Anaphora needs at least three repetitions to build — two is just coincidence.

---

### Pain-Insight-Action (Dan Koe)
**Best for:** Long-form newsletters, deep threads, content that builds trust and converts over time

1. **Pain** — Start with a relatable, specific struggle. Create the "Me too!" moment immediately. The reader must see themselves in the problem.
2. **Insight** — Reframe the problem. Deliver the "Aha!" — a new lens that the reader hasn't considered. This is not advice. It's a perspective shift that makes the problem look fundamentally different.
3. **Action** — Concrete, executable "how-to" the reader can implement today. Not "think about X." Do X. Transforms the piece from entertainment to utility.

*Craft note:* The Insight step is the intellectual core of everything Koe writes. Don't rush to Action before the reframe has fully landed.

---

### Zero-Click Micro-Story (Amanda Natividad)
**Best for:** Platform-native posts that build algorithmic capital and brand affinity — no CTA required

1. **Thesis Statement** — Clear, compelling main point upfront. No burying the lead.
2. **Narrative Arc** — Beginning, middle, and end — fully contained within the post itself.
3. **Standalone Punchline** — Deliver the full value in bullet points. The reader gets the complete idea without clicking anything.
4. **(Optional) Rant Lead** — Lead with a strong, potentially controversial POV to gauge interest before the deeper content lands.

*Craft note:* The post must be defensible as a complete intellectual unit. No teasing. No "read more for the good stuff." Give away the best information freely — authority is built through generosity. Use this framework for roughly half of all social output to build algorithmic capital; the other half can carry a CTA.

---

### BPAS (Dan Koe)
**Best for:** Short, high-impact persuasive posts on any platform

1. **Big Idea** — State the conclusion or bold claim immediately. Top-down structure — don't make the reader wait for the point.
2. **Problem** — Identify the specific friction or failure state the reader is in.
3. **Amplify** — Give concrete, specific examples of how the problem impacts their real life: their relationships, their sleep, their income, their mornings. Raise the emotional stakes with specificity. "How does chronic fatigue affect your marriage?" beats "Tired people underperform."
4. **Solution** — The actionable answer or reframe.

*Craft note:* Amplification is the differentiator between a post that scrolls and one that stops. Generic problems get skipped. Specific consequences land.

---

### Pyramid Principle (Dan Koe)
**Best for:** Logic-driven persuasion, educational posts, framework or system explanations

1. **Conclusion First** — State the big idea immediately so the reader knows within one sentence whether it's relevant to them.
2. **Key Arguments** — 3+ supporting points that independently prove the conclusion.
3. **Support** — Facts, data, anecdotes, or quotes backing each argument.

*Craft note:* No slow burns. No "first, let me give you some context." The conclusion is line one. Each argument should stand alone — not just continue the narrative.

---

### 5-4-3-2-1 (Justin Welsh — Landing Pages)
**Best for:** Sales pages, offer pages, newsletter signup pages

- **5 Key Benefits** — Specific outcomes the buyer achieves. Not features — outcomes. "You'll close your first client in 30 days" not "Access to 6 modules."
- **4 Common Objections** — Address time, money, efficacy, and relevance head-on.
- **3 Testimonials** — Social proof that speaks directly to the objections above.
- **2 Pricing Options** — Use price anchoring to make both feel digestible.
- **1 Irresistible Bonus** — A complementary asset that tips the final conversion decision.

*Craft note:* Consider reversing to 1-2-3-4-5 to build curiosity and handle objections before the full value is revealed. Test both directions.

## Step 4: Layer in Masters-Level Composition

Regardless of framework, apply these techniques in the final draft.

**Formatting**
- 1-3-1 structure for LinkedIn/X: one bold opening → three supporting lines → one closing hook or CTA.
- Single-line paragraphs. White space reduces friction on mobile — it is not wasted space.
- No dense text blocks. Ever.

**Rhythm**
- Staccato prose: short sentences. Then longer ones that build and carry momentum. Then short again.
- Vary sentence length intentionally — monotone rhythm kills engagement.
- Active voice throughout. Cut passive constructions on sight.

**Psychology**
- Curiosity Gaps: hooks that promise a "secret," "rule," or insight without immediately delivering it. Triggers the Zeigarnik Effect — the brain's drive to resolve open loops.
- Pattern Interrupts: challenge a mental model the reader holds. Make them stop mid-scroll.
- The Say-Do Gap: ask the reader to audit whether their actions match their stated priorities — creates immediate cognitive dissonance that compels resolution.

**Language**
- Anaphora: repeat a word or phrase at the start of 3–5+ successive sentences for rhythmic persuasive momentum.
- Visceral language: replace passive description with sensory, active imagery.
- Specificity over generality: specific numbers, names, and situations stick. Vague claims slide off.

---

## Step 5: Red Flag Test

Run this before finalizing any output:

- Does the opening line demand attention — or explain context? → If it explains, rewrite it.
- Could this have been written by anyone? → If yes, add specificity until it couldn't.
- Is there any hedging ("seems," "appears," "might")? → Cut it.
- Does the agitation or amplification step do real work? → One line and move on is not enough.
- Does it sound human — or like a template with the blanks filled in? → If the latter, find what's making it feel generated and fix it.

---

## Output Format

Deliver in this order:

1. **Framework selected** + one-line rationale
2. **Draft copy** — fully composed, not a template. Real sentences, real voice.
3. **Optional: 1–2 variants** when meaningfully different approaches exist (different angles, not just different tones)
4. **Brief craft note** (2–3 sentences max) — flag any trade-offs or what would sharpen it with more context

The copy is the work. Keep everything else tight.
